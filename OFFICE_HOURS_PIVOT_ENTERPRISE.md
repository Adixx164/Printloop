**Assessment:** 🔴 RED — do not proceed with "pivot to enterprise"

### Critical Issues:

**Q1 - Problem:** Same as seed round. You're describing the **student problem**, not an enterprise problem. "Pivot to enterprise" implies selling to companies/universities as buyers (B2B), not students (B2C). The enterprise buyer (university admin, facilities manager, procurement) has a completely different problem: "We can't manage print vendor contracts, students complain, we have no visibility into spend, maintenance is a nightmare."

**Q2 - Customer:** You named a **student** (Hilay Austin), not an enterprise buyer. Enterprise ICP = "Mr. Adebayo, Director of Student Affairs, Nile University, manages 12 print vendors, ₦2M/month spend, gets complaints daily, needs centralized billing."

**Q3 - Distribution:** WhatsApp/IG/TikTok targets **students**, not enterprise buyers. Enterprise distribution = "Cold email to 50 Directors of Student Affairs at Nigerian universities" or "Referral from UNILAG's ICT Director" or "NUC conference booth."

**Q4 - Defensibility:** You listed distribution channels again, not a moat. Enterprise moats = **contract lock-in** (3-year university contracts), **integration moat** (deep LMS/SIS integration), **procurement moat** (approved vendor status).

**Q5 - Capital:** Same 15M NGN. Enterprise sales cycles are 6-18 months. 2-year payback is aggressive for enterprise. You need runway for 12-18 months before first contract closes.

**Q6 - Founder Fit:** Same obsession — but enterprise sales requires different skills (long-cycle selling, procurement navigation, contract negotiation). Are you ready for that?

---

### Verdict:
This isn't a "pivot to enterprise" — it's the same B2C student model with enterprise words. 

**Two paths forward:**

1. **Stay B2C (students)** — Double down on seed round brief. Fix Q2/Q3 there. Your traction is with students.

2. **True enterprise pivot** — Re-run office hours with:
   - Problem: University admin's words
   - Customer: Named Director of Student Affairs
   - Distribution: Enterprise sales motion
   - Defensibility: Contract/integration moats
   - Capital: 18-month runway for sales cycle
   - Fit: Are you ready to sell to bureaucrats?
